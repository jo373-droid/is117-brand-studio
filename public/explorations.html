Same product / same words

Two ways to say it.

These are early directions for Lukewarm, a relaxed, contrarian Creator brand. The plain white T-shirt and offer stay the same so the visual choices are easier to compare.

WHITE TEE — CLASSROOM DEMO

Reference & choices

Herbert Bayer, Architektur Lichtbilder Vortrag Professor Hans Poelzig, 1926, MoMA. A clear grid, asymmetrical type and one accent color organize the same offer.

Intended effect & risk

Should make product information quick to scan for both audiences. Risk: too orderly to communicate the contrarian personality.

B / PostmodernistMixing · interruption · play

Lukewarm / 001

Simple shirt.
Your point of view.

An ordinary white tee, ready for the way you wear it.

See the T-shirt ↗ WELL, MAYBE.

Reference & choices

Ettore Sottsass, Carlton Room Divider, 1981, manufactured by Memphis, MoMA. Playful geometry, bright accents and a deliberate collision of visual cues inform this direction.

Intended effect & risk

Should feel memorable and comfortably rule-breaking for the creative-style visitor. Risk: layered decoration could distract from the offer or size information.

Held constant in both: “Simple shirt. Your point of view.” / “An ordinary white tee, ready for the way you wear it.” / “See the T-shirt.” The supporting line describes a styling invitation, not a verified fit or fabric claim.

These previews are a comparison, not a selected direction. The modernist notes draw on MoMA’s description of Bayer’s use of sans-serif type, a grid and asymmetry; the postmodernist notes draw on the visible “Carlton” room divider and MoMA’s framing of postmodernism as eclectic mixing of styles.
